# Code of conduct

## Where this applies

Issues, discussions, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere somebody is
speaking for this project. It applies to everyone equally, maintainer included —
a code of conduct that exempts whoever wrote it is a statement about power, not
about behaviour.

## What this project is for

Arguing about rules. [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) asks for the rule that
did not hold up in practice and what it cost, and
[`rationale.md`](method/rationale.md) keeps a standing list of where the method
is most likely wrong. **Telling a maintainer they are wrong is the contribution
this project wants most.** Nothing below is meant to make that harder, and
anybody using this document to deflect a substantive criticism is misusing it.

The distinction the whole page rests on: *this rule is wrong, and here is the
failure it produces* is the work. *You are careless* is not a sharper version of
the same thing — it is a different thing, and it ends the conversation the first
one was starting.

## What is expected

- **Argue about the claim.** A rule, a check, a wrong sentence, a missing case.
- **Say what you actually observed**, and separate it from what you concluded.
  The difference matters more here than usual, because much of this repository
  is claims about tools that behave differently across versions.
- **Assume the other person read carefully and disagrees anyway.** Most of the
  time that is what happened.
- **Let a decision be a decision.** A maintainer who says no and gives a reason
  has answered you. Re-opening the same argument in a new issue is not
  persistence, it is a cost imposed on somebody else.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, sustained personal hostility, or aggression directed at a person
  rather than a claim.
- Insults or dismissal based on who somebody is — nationality, ethnicity, sex,
  gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, appearance, or
  first language. **English is the language of this repository and the second or
  third language of many people reading it.** Correcting somebody's English
  where the meaning was clear is unwelcome; treating imperfect English as
  evidence about the argument is squarely in this list.
- Publishing anybody's private information, or content that was shared with you
  privately.
- Sexual attention or imagery.
- Deliberately wasting people's time: contributions you know are wrong,
  arguments made to provoke, or bad-faith reports under this document.

## Reporting

Email **nanatsusaya-grimora@gmx.net**. Include what happened, where, and roughly
when. A link is enough; you do not owe anybody a summary of something
unpleasant.

**Reporting a maintainer is a legitimate use of this address.** So is asking
whether something is worth reporting before deciding — an answer costs a few
minutes and is not held against you.

## What happens then

One maintainer, so no committee and no pretence of one:

1. **Acknowledgement within a week.** If a week passes with nothing, assume it
   did not arrive rather than that it was ignored — send it again.
2. **The report stays private.** Your name is not passed on without your
   agreement, including to the person reported. Where a response would identify
   you regardless, you are told that before anything happens.
3. **A decision, with the reasoning.** Typically: nothing warranted, a private
   word, a public correction, or a block. What was decided goes back to you.

Not every report ends in action. One that does not still gets an answer saying
so and why, because a report answered with silence is indistinguishable from one
that never arrived.

## What this document does not do

It does not make this a safe place to be wrong in public — no document does
that; the behaviour of the people here does. It does not settle technical
disagreements, and it is not a route to winning one. And it cannot help with
anything outside the places named at the top.
